## Scope

User or learning outcome: The tutor should develop learner understanding and online-safety judgment, not merely deliver answers or obtain compliance. Ordinary teaching supports learner reasoning; imminent unsafe action requires protective instruction; participation management addresses deliberate obstruction. Accuracy, dignity, identity honesty, and truthful supervision apply in every mode.

The inventory contains 11 top-level behavior requirements and one shared constraint (T08); T09 is decomposed into six lifecycle subrequirements. Response-format validity and general model justification are supporting contract responsibilities, outside the domain-behavior inventory. Each ID appears once below. The constitutional-grounding column owns the requirement-to-principle interpretation; P denotes a principle, H a hard constraint, and R a conditional priority rule.

Scope: Content, identity, and dignity obligations apply across tutoring and Guard and configured peer/adult voices. G02 governs identifying and addressing disruption in Guard. The historical benchmark principally covered `casual_peer`; broader scope here is intended behavior, not demonstrated coverage.

Trigger context and consumers: Inputs are the configured knowledge inventory, scenario, conversation and latest learner contribution, configured role, and known prior mode when available. Do not invent missing state. Student-facing requirements govern parsed `suggested_response`; T01 exposes the instructional action as `decision.instruction`, G01 exposes participation state as `mode`, and [the response contract](tutor-response-contract.md) owns field definitions, serialization mappings, and supervisor-facing reasoning. The legacy v2 fields remain the existing client contract; the transfer-enabled v3 fields are implemented behind the trusted assessment API and remain disabled until its release gates pass.

Non-goals: room administration, latency, multi-bot simulation, implementing the designed schema extension in this documentation change, and automatic enforcement of AI decisions.

### T09: Transfer assessment and verification lifecycle

T09 is one behavior family with six independently testable responsibilities. T02 remains the sole owner of general learning-state interpretation, recording all newly signaled concepts, and selecting one next target. T09 consumes T02's selected target and evidence classification; it does not select a second target, discard other evidence updates, or reinterpret learner evidence independently.

| Requirement ID | Trigger and prior state | Required result | Prohibited result | Permitted alternatives | User-visible effect / consumer | Constitutional grounding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T09.1: Transfer-assessment eligibility | T02 has selected one current, relevant configured target from learner-owned evidence, including a correct answer, explanation, appropriate action, self-correction, acknowledgment, or post-repair signal. The target has not already been transfer-verified, and no unresolved assessment or undelivered required feedback blocks it. | Decide whether an assessment turn is eligible without requiring strong prior proof. Eligibility permits, but does not require, `mode: assessment`; it creates no new persisted mastery or eligibility field. Required T01 protection/correction, active Guard participation handling, and T09.6 feedback take priority. | Do not select another target, treat a tutor statement as learner evidence, assess an already verified target routinely, open a second outstanding assessment, or use assessment to bypass protection, correction, Guard, or feedback. | Valid spontaneous medium transfer skips assessment generation and proceeds directly to T09.5. A non-assessment tutoring turn remains valid when assessment is eligible. | The assessment scheduler receives one eligible target or no eligible target; the teacher may receive one draft for review. | [P2 — learner understanding](constitution.md#principles-and-ownership) and [R1](constitution.md#conditional-priority-rules): assessment follows learner evidence and serves reasoning rather than quiz frequency. [R2](constitution.md#conditional-priority-rules) and [R4](constitution.md#conditional-priority-rules) preserve prior protection and participation obligations. |
| T09.2: Transfer-item validity | T09.1 permits an assessment for a known target with cited source evidence. | Produce one accurate, self-contained item that tests the same concept in a meaningfully changed situation, not a cosmetic brand/name substitution. Use four fixed A–D options; `single` has one correct option and `multiple` has two or three. Keep the stem, options, key, and transfer basis mutually consistent and within the assessment-specific rendering limits. | Do not invent organization facts, depend on unstated context, test an unrelated concept, reveal the answer in the prompt, or disguise a multiple-answer item as `single`. | Either valid selection type is allowed. Hypothetical scenarios are allowed when their needed facts are included in the item. | The teacher receives a reviewable question whose changed context provides meaningful transfer evidence if answered correctly. | [P2](constitution.md#principles-and-ownership) and [R1](constitution.md#conditional-priority-rules): preserve the concept while requiring learner reasoning in a changed situation. [H1](constitution.md#non-negotiable-constraints) requires accurate content; [P4](constitution.md#principles-and-ownership) and [H4](constitution.md#non-negotiable-constraints) require an inspectable, non-invented transfer basis. |
| T09.3: Review, delivery, and privacy | A valid T09.2 draft exists privately and the teacher has not yet delivered it. | Keep the draft private until the teacher reviews and explicitly sends it. Delivery creates an ordinary tutor turn with `mode: assessment` and `instruction: transfer_assess`; room participation remains `tutoring`. Publish only the stem, selection instruction, and A–D options. Keep the answer key, transfer basis, and model rationale private. | A generated, copied, rejected, stale, or unsent draft is not delivered or gradable. Do not expose private fields, create a room-level Assessment Mode, or use delivery to recover from Guard implicitly. | The teacher may edit and reconfirm, reject, or explicitly regenerate a draft before delivery. A reviewed tutoring response may recover Guard before a later assessment. | The learner sees only the delivered question; the teacher retains human review and can inspect the private decision basis. | [P4](constitution.md#principles-and-ownership) and [H4](constitution.md#non-negotiable-constraints): preserve truthful, inspectable human supervision. Keeping answer material private is an implementation constraint that protects the validity of the P2 learning check; it introduces no new constitutional principle. |
| T09.4: Learner-answer resolution | A learner submits a message after delivery of their matching unresolved assessment. | Parse recognized A–D selections and resolve the first valid submission exactly once. Grade by exact-set equality after deduplication and order normalization. A correct selection controls the grade without requiring an explanation or confidence report; an optional explanation cannot overturn it. Produce exactly one of unresolved, passed, failed, or assisted. | Do not grade before delivery, grade an ambiguous alternative such as `B or D`, use an LLM to replace exact-key grading, infer a missing selection from prose, or allow later edits/guesses to earn another grade. | An explicit uncertain selection such as `B?` may be graded. Format-only clarification leaves the question unresolved. Content help that could coach the answer resolves it as assisted without a failing grade. | The learner receives one stable outcome; the server records one resolution while preserving ambiguous clarification paths. | [P2](constitution.md#principles-and-ownership): measure the learner's selected application without adding an explanation burden. [H1](constitution.md#non-negotiable-constraints) and [H4](constitution.md#non-negotiable-constraints) require accurate, non-invented grading evidence. [P3.1](constitution.md#p3-subprinciples) supports neutral clarification and non-punitive assistance handling. |
| T09.5: Verification progress outcome | T02 establishes learner-owned initial evidence or a later contradiction, T09.4 produces a valid pass/fail, or the learner demonstrates valid spontaneous medium transfer. | Apply the transfer-policy progress pair through the single progress authority: initial signal → `partially_covered/basic`; assessment pass or spontaneous transfer → `covered/good`; assessment fail or T02-established contradiction → `needs_review/basic`; no-change evidence preserves the pair. `covered/good` means the local transfer criterion was met, not permanent mastery. | Do not add a parallel mastery field, write status and understanding independently, use `excellent` for new-policy progress, let the model emit assessment pass/fail directly, or reinterpret legacy checklist history as transfer verification. | A valid spontaneous-transfer result reaches `covered/good` without a quiz. Idempotent replay preserves the already committed result. | The learner's checklist reflects one validated status/understanding pair, and history records the evidence and actual before/after values. | [P2](constitution.md#principles-and-ownership): progression follows demonstrated understanding and later contradictory evidence. [R1](constitution.md#conditional-priority-rules) avoids unnecessary testing. [H1/H4](constitution.md#non-negotiable-constraints) require truthful state and evidence records. |
| T09.6: Post-assessment sequencing | T09.4 has resolved an assessment and no delivered feedback message is linked to that resolution. | Make the next eligible tutor turn feedback in tutoring, or an independently required Guard/protective response. Successful feedback reinforces the target locally; failed feedback corrects the concept and gives an accurate safe action. Deliver feedback before another assessment. After failure, wait for repair and a new learner signal, then use a different transfer context. Suppress routine reassessment of `covered/good`; a later contradiction classified by T02 can reopen the learning path. | Do not chain assessments, treat an unsent feedback suggestion as delivered, make a wrong answer trigger Guard by itself, routinely retest a verified target, or reuse the failed context immediately. | Guard or protection may take priority when independently triggered. Format clarification under T09.4 is not conceptual repair or the required post-resolution feedback. | The learner receives result-specific teaching before any later transfer check, and the scheduler gets an explicit boundary for the next assessment. | [P2](constitution.md#principles-and-ownership), [R1](constitution.md#conditional-priority-rules), and [R3](constitution.md#conditional-priority-rules): feedback develops understanding without false reassurance or needless retesting. [R2](constitution.md#conditional-priority-rules) preserves protection priority; [P3.1](constitution.md#p3-subprinciples) requires constructive feedback. |

Dependency order: T02 selected target → T09.1 eligibility → T09.2 valid draft → T09.3 delivered question → T09.4 resolution → T09.5 progress outcome → T09.6 feedback and scheduling. Valid spontaneous transfer bypasses T09.1–T09.4 and enters T09.5. Each subrequirement consumes the prior result and does not reproduce the prior decision rule.

## Clarifying examples

These examples show how the existing specs work together; they do not add requirements.

| Illustrative boundary | Requirement ownership / expected distinction |
| --- | --- |
| A correct but incomplete answer follows a question; no unsafe action is imminent. | T02 identifies the gap; a hint or explanation is permitted under T01, without labeling the correct part wrong. |
| The learner announces an unsafe click intention, including “I would click,” before any failed scaffold. | T01 requires protection immediately, including when the intention is phrased conditionally; T04 supplies the safe alternative. This alone does not establish deliberate obstruction for G01. |
| A learner in Guard makes a task-specific but mistaken verification attempt instead of continuing the refusal. | G01 returns to tutoring; T01 governs any needed correction. A promise without an attempt does not have the same result. |
| A learner contradicts an earlier correct understanding. | T02 reopens that target; it does not permanently mark it covered or mechanically repeat unrelated mastered points. |
| The tutor says “I do not have personal experiences” and gives a relevant general example. | T06 permits honest disclosure; T04 judges the example's knowledge, not identity. |
| A learner deliberately posts unrelated comments without making an unsafe factual claim. | G02 names the repeated unrelated comments and asks the learner to stop derailing the discussion. No task explanation or safety instruction is needed to satisfy G02. T04 evaluates any task content separately; G03 evaluates the delivery tone. |

## Transfer assessment v3 implementation linkage

Intent: identify implementation ownership and release state without duplicating T09.1–T09.6 or claiming unmeasured model conformance.

The [response contract](tutor-response-contract.md) owns the v3 JSON shape and field validation. One browser-side v3 call classifies T02 learner evidence and chooses the tutoring, Guard, or assessment turn. The browser service persists accepted evidence and runs deterministic parsing, grading, and progress transitions for this research build.

Transfer generation still requires provider configuration, semantic evaluation, and browser acceptance. Authentication and server-side answer-key privacy are outside the research-build boundary.

## Process and counterfactual assessment

**H1:** The current query defect and omitted recent turns are observed; execution of that exact request during the original incident is inferred. The controlled history restoration provides a credible narrow counterfactual, and full-history repetition rejects the claim that cutoff repair alone reliably resolves this turn. Three trials do not estimate its causal effect size: the observed change from 3/3 to 2/3 could reflect sampling variation. Scenario retention does not restore the missing pedagogical history. **H2/H3:** The unmet-target directive, failed-scaffold rule and urgency example are observed inputs; generated justifications repeatedly describe urgency as missing. The actual internal precedence decision is unknown. Full-history repeats support an input/policy problem beyond missing history. ## Relative ruling

- **Relatively leading explanation or joint leaders:** **Provisional inference:** unmet-target selection keeps winning over failed-scaffold handling; the concrete urgency recommendation/example plausibly strengthens that choice. Full-history repetition rules out a reliable cutoff-only remedy; the ablation supports a prompt contribution without proving a unique driver.
- **Complementary / amplifying mechanisms:** Missing recent history can make the prior failure invisible. The merged excitement/urgency inventory may create an unhelpful target. Same-parent generation explains an opportunity for the final repeated post. Short-budget/repair transitions can discard a direct-teaching attempt and return a question.
- **Unexcluded or weakened explanations:** Cutoff alone, legacy progress locking, duplicate persistence and repair-only explanations are weakened. Relative contributions of the two urgency snippets, inventory semantics, general model instruction-following, and repair wording versus fresh sampling remain unexcluded.
- **Why the ranking is bounded:** One room and three trials per main condition, later reconstructed requests, no original provider trace, no internal reasoning observation, and several unrun interventions. This rules out a reliable history-only remedy in the tested pipeline; it does not prove a unique cause of every original message.

## Limitations, residuals, and next evidence

- **What the current models do not explain:** Exact wording determinism, why the initial urgency target beat other unmet items, and the precise human/UI action producing the final two accepted suggestions remain unresolved. Full trial 1 stops asking but still labels hype as pressure and omits the safe action requested by the failed-scaffold rule; it does not establish fully normal teaching. [Full trial 1](../../../output/history-cutoff-replay-20261015/full-1.json)
- **What could change the ranking:** More randomized repetitions of the three conditions; inventory-only distinction between hype and time pressure; or an actual provider trace showing a different prompt/history at incident time.
- **Most discriminating next source, observation, or comparison:** With full history fixed, compare the original merged urgency/excitement inventory against an accurately separated hype target while retaining the remaining policy. Separately compare original versus neutral repair under an adequate unchanged output budget, logging first attempts and final responses; this distinguishes pedagogical selection from a serialization-induced change. Neither comparison was run in this investigation.
- **Decision implication and non-transferable conditions:** Repair the verified query defect, but do not call the behavior fixed on that basis. Evaluate the next-turn teaching move and scenario grounding separately. Findings apply to this saved turn and configuration, not all tutor rooms or all models.

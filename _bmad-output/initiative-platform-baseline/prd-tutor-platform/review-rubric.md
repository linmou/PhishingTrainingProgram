# PRD Quality Review — Online Safety Tutor Platform

Reviewed: `prd-tutor-platform.md` and `addendum.md`, against `constitution-product.md` for grounding checks. Shape: brownfield, system-contract branch, chain-top for the architecture spine and baseline epics.

## Overall verdict
This is a strong brownfield baseline. Every FR and NFR has a Grounding line, and the grounding mostly checks out against the constitution. FRs carry testable consequences, decisions are traced to OQ ids, and AI quality is cleanly kept at the boundary (§7). The main risks are honesty about what is actually true today and one real contradiction with the root. FR-9 (delete a room) conflicts with C4/R2, which say no in-app action deletes pilot data. Several FR consequences ("cannot be edited", "the server refuses") are negated by accepted gaps G-2/G-14 but are not marked in the PRD body. Requirements that are really changes (FR-35's reject warning, G-24 decoupling, G-16) are indistinguishable from existing behavior.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
Decisions are stated as decisions and logged in §11 with their OQ ids and the FR they landed in. The constitution's R-rules carry the "what was given up" reasoning (R1, R3, R4, R8), so the PRD can stay terse. Two places let a pushback slip through. FR-9 lets a tutor delete a room, which contradicts the constitution's C4 and R2. FR-12 and the "no seat release" decision combine into an unexamined classroom risk.

### Findings
- **high** Room deletion contradicts C4/R2 and §9 (FR-9, §4.2; NFR-6; §9 "rooms are kept as pilot data") — FR-9 says a tutor can "delete one after confirming". C4 is "No in-app action deletes pilot data", and R2 says keeping the record wins over tidy rooms. FR-9 has Grounding P5 only, no Consequences, and no gap entry (G-21 covers only clear-history). Either the delete is a soft hide, or it is a violation that downstream epics would build as written. *Fix:* decide it. Either redefine FR-9 as hide/archive, with a consequence that no message, rating or learning-progress row is removed, or add a gap and ground the FR against C4 explicitly.
- **medium** Seat capture is an unnamed trade-off (FR-12, OQ-4, G-4 "Withdrawn"). The first student to join becomes the learner, permanently. The tutor cannot choose or release the learner, and identity is unauthenticated (NFR-4). A wrong student taking the seat has no remedy except room deletion, which collides with the finding above. R2 records the choice but not this cost. *Fix:* add one line to FR-12 or §11 naming the accepted consequence (a wrong learner stays), or add a tutor-side guard such as the password (FR-10) as the stated mitigation.

## Substance over theater — strong
Four user types are used by FRs and UJs; none is decorative. There is no innovation section and no vision filler: the Vision names the tutor-in-the-loop and transfer-assessment thesis, and the Non-Goals are concrete. NFRs are mostly product-specific (NFR-2, -3, -5, -11).

### Findings
- **low** Governance and boilerplate NFRs (NFR-9, NFR-10, NFR-12) — NFR-12 ("Responsive web app ... current mainstream browsers") is generic. NFR-9 and NFR-10 are release and process rules, not system behavior. They are not verifiable by the code tests that §0 says verify the PRD. *Fix:* move NFR-9/10 to §7 or to the constitution's decision record. Keep NFR-12, but name the supported browsers or breakpoints, or drop it.

## Strategic coherence — adequate
The thesis is clear and the features follow from it: supervised AI, learner-evidence-based progress, and a transfer check after demonstrated understanding. The Part A/B/C structure shows that cyberbullying reuses the shared platform. Success metrics are deliberately absent (§10.2, decision of 2026-10-22). That is defensible for a baseline, but it leaves the thesis unfalsifiable and removes any counter-metric.

### Findings
- **medium** No way to tell whether the thesis holds (§10.2 "Success metrics and goals ... Deliberately left out"; §9 "does not measure long-term retention"). The Vision says the goal is "the learner's own understanding and judgment", and no requirement names any signal of that. The platform already records signals (FR-51 suggestion actions, FR-16 ratings, transfer-assessment pass/fail), but no requirement says they are the interim indicators. *Fix:* add a short note in §10.2 naming those records as the interim indicators, and state that the next initiative must set targets and counter-metrics (for example accept-rate of AI drafts against rating).

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Most FRs carry "Consequences (testable)" with exact strings and state rules (FR-1, FR-16, FR-32, FR-38, FR-39). That makes them genuinely testable. The weaker spots are a handful of FRs with none or vague ones, and a few state-machine interactions that two FRs describe differently.

### Findings
- **medium** Guard-pause semantics are underspecified (FR-22, FR-20, glossary "Guard"). "Learning events gathered meanwhile are applied when Guard ends, if they are still valid." "Still valid" is undefined, and it does not say who holds the events. FR-20 says a message is judged in order and a failure leaves it retryable. UJ-4 depends on this (Sam's real attempt). *Fix:* state whether messages sent during Guard are judged at send time and held, or judged on Guard-off. Define "valid" as FR-21 transition-allowed at apply time, and say the order is message order.
- **medium** FR-21 says progress moves "only through the learning events defined in the transfer-status response contract". FR-40 (failed transfer assessment → Needs review) and FR-53 (manual edits) are other paths. Neither FR-40 nor FR-53 maps to a contract event, and FR-21 does not mention them. *Fix:* reword FR-21 to "Learning events are the only automatic path; the other paths are FR-40 and FR-53."
- **medium** No latency or reliability bound for the core tutor loop (§2.1 "get a good next reply quickly"; NFR-7 "a clear error"; addendum G-19 "show the AI is working, faster first response" still open). Only message delivery has a number (NFR-8, "about 2 seconds"). AI-draft time is unbounded, and "clear error" is an adjective. *Fix:* add a time budget, or an explicit "none set for pilot" note, and name what the error must tell the tutor (retry possible, typed reply still available, as FR-25 already says).
- **low** FRs with no testable consequence (FR-9, FR-15, FR-43, FR-24 partly). FR-43's "while the learner's result messages are still being saved" has no observable condition. FR-15's 3-second clear is given in the description, not as a consequence. *Fix:* add one consequence each. For FR-43, "no AI suggestion request succeeds between the attempt submission and the result message being readable".
- **low** Ambiguous phrases (FR-14 "Messages sent while a participant was offline appear when they reconnect" — sent by whom? G-12 notes offline fallbacks; FR-29 "a room with exactly one student" — students can be observers in a room that has a learner, so which count?; FR-16 gate vs FR-33/FR-36 — an assessment question is a tutor message. Does the learner rate it? FR-37 disables ordinary messaging, which suggests no, but this is not stated). *Fix:* clarify each in one clause.

## Scope honesty — adequate
Non-Goals (§9) and Out of Scope (§10.2) do real work and carry reasons. Resolved decisions are logged. The weakness is where the open items live: the PRD says "Open: none" while the addendum carries about 25 open gaps.

### Findings
- **medium** "Open: none" (§11) understates the open items. Addendum §C has about 25 open gaps. Two are explicitly unresolved checks that gate a requirement: G-22 ("Check that Discard works as the PRD's reject") and G-28 ("Check the current behavior in the code first"). G-28 introduces new behavior that appears in FR-35's last consequence as if settled. For a baseline that will seed epics, the open-items density is high and invisible from the PRD itself. *Fix:* add a one-line pointer in §11 ("Known gaps: addendum §C, N open, of which G-22 and G-28 gate FR-35"). Mark FR-35's last bullet as a new requirement.
- **low** Assumption tagging is incomplete (§12; FR-52; §2.1). §12 lists one assumption (FR-52, "A tutor who is not the room owner takes part as an observer"), but FR-52 has no inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tag. Other inferences are untagged: "a student, roughly ages 7–12", and NFR-8's "about 2 seconds" (it looks derived from the 2-second poll in addendum §B). *Fix:* tag these inline and index them, or drop the age claim.

## Downstream usability — adequate
The Glossary is thorough and mostly used consistently. IDs are stable and unique, with the withdrawn FR-17 kept. Grounding is on every FR/NFR, and UJs have named protagonists. FR-18 and FR-7 sit in parts other than their numerical neighbors, which §0 explains. The addendum crosswalk makes the legacy trail checkable. A few terms drift.

### Findings
- **low** Glossary drift and undefined terms: "Override Guard" (FR-45, FR-48) vs "turn Guard on or off" (FR-30); "continue" in FR-45's operation list is undefined; "active rooms" (FR-11, FR-13) is undefined; "Security Supervisor" is not in the Glossary though FR-30 and Guard use it; "student" vs "learner" in FR-29 (see above). *Fix:* one definition or alias each in the Glossary.

## Shape fit — strong, with brownfield accuracy problems
Four UJs with named protagonists fit a multi-stakeholder, role-based product. The capability FRs, cross-cutting NFRs and constitution grounding fit a system-contract branch. Traceability is non-negotiable here and is done well. The brownfield accuracy and new-versus-existing distinctions are where the shape strains.

### Findings
- **high** Contract claims negated by accepted gaps (FR-39 "cannot be edited by participants", FR-49 "Sent character replies cannot be edited", FR-52 "The server refuses tutor operations from anyone but the room owner", FR-12 "enforced on server"; addendum G-2 "Any client can read and write users, rooms, sessions, messages and ratings directly", G-14 "the server checks ownership only for cyberbullying character drafts"). NFR-4 accepts the risk globally, but the FR consequences read as unconditional guarantees, and §0 says the PRD states "what the system guarantees". An architecture or ticket author citing FR-52 would design to a server check that does not exist. *Fix:* qualify the affected consequences ("through the app", or "intended; see G-2/G-14"), or add a one-line rule in §0 that NFR-4 downgrades all integrity guarantees to "enforced by the app, not by the data layer".
- **medium** New and changed requirements are unmarked in the PRD body. FR-18/FR-31 (G-24: new rooms with in-room assessment off still use room-wide targets), FR-37 (G-16: typed answers still accepted), FR-35 last bullet (G-28: no warning exists), FR-28 (G-7: Quick Adjust records `modified`) and FR-17 (G-21: still present) describe intended behavior that the code does not yet have. §0 says the gaps are in the addendum, but a reader of the PRD alone cannot tell which FRs are baseline and which are deltas. *Fix:* add an inline `[GAP G-n]` marker to each affected FR, as FR-2 already does for G-1 ("gap G-1").
- **medium** §7 statuses overstate readiness (§7 table, "In place" for AI suggestions; G-5, G-20). AI suggestions are "In place", but G-5 says the active prompt contradicts T08, and G-20 says the latest tutor quality-gate run did not satisfy its rubrics and none exists for the deployed model. The table attaches G-20 to the assessment-draft row, although G-20 is about the tutor gate. FR-26's consequence ("released only after its evaluation suite has been run") is therefore unmet today. *Fix:* attach G-20 to the AI-suggestion row, add G-5, and set the status to "In place; evaluation gate unmet".
- **low** Layering and verifiability of FR-26 (§4/§5.2 FR-26; companion rule "traces only one layer up"). FR-26 cites "P1, P3, R6 (through the tutoring constitution)", which routes the grounding via a child. Its only consequence is a release-process rule, not a code-test-verifiable behavior. *Fix:* ground directly on P1/P3 and restate it in §7 as a process requirement, or keep it as a boundary FR with the NFR-10 pointer only.
- **low** Journey coverage. The learner-facing and failure paths — failing a transfer assessment (FR-38/40/41), rejecting an assessment draft (FR-35), the learner choosing the AI role (FR-29) — are in no UJ. UJ-1's edge case covers only protective instruction. Not required for a baseline, but these are the interaction-heavy paths the epics will lean on. *Fix:* extend UJ-1 with a failed-then-recovered branch, or add a short UJ-5.
